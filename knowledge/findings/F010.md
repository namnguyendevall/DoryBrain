# Finding 010: Accurate One-Step Prediction Optimizes Transients but Cannot Shift Boundaries

## Overview
Batch 0007 evaluated E008, an actor equipped with a **One-step Predictive Internal Model**. Unlike estimators (like E007) that only tracked beliefs about trends, E008 actively *learned a local causal transition model* of the environment (estimating `rest_gain` and `work_cost`) and used it to simulate the consequences of candidate actions one step into the future before executing them. 

## 5-Tier Evaluation

### Tier 1 — Implementation
- **Question**: Does the actor maintain an internal predictive model?
- **Metric**: `estimated_rest_gain` and `estimated_work_cost` in internal state.
- **Expected**: **YES**. E008 successfully implemented the capability to store and update causal physics parameters.

### Tier 2 — Learning
- **Question**: Does the actor accurately learn the true environmental physics?
- **Metrics**: 
  - `prediction_MAE` (Error between predicted next resource and actual next resource)
  - `parameter_error` (Error between estimated parameters and actual physics)
- **Result**: **YES**. At the comatose boundary (`gain_01`), E008 demonstrated a remarkably low `prediction_MAE` of `1.53` and a `parameter_error` of exactly `1.00`. The 1.00 parameter error is highly significant: the actor perfectly estimated the *net* transitions but folded the constant passive decay (-1) directly into its rest gain and work cost parameters. It accurately captured the effective local physics. The E008_Control, fed with pseudo-observations, exhibited high prediction error (`MAE: 5.21`).

### Tier 3 — Engagement
- **Question**: Does the actor use the model to make decisions?
- **Metric**: `prediction_usage_fraction`.
- **Result**: **YES**. Following a brief forced-exploration warmup (1 rest, 1 work) to initialize parameters—which is part of the pre-registered policy and isolated from the predictive decision phase—E008 relied entirely on forward simulation for its decisions, logging a `100.0%` usage fraction. Since the actor deterministically chooses `argmax U` post-warmup, its counterfactual loss $\Delta U$ (Utility of best predicted action vs chosen action) is strictly $0$, confirming optimal local usage of its model.

### Tier 4 — Behavioral Change
- **Question**: Does the predictive model alter the actor's trajectory?
- **Metrics**: Transient survival and work rate.
- **Result**: **YES**. By relying on greedy consequence anticipation, E008 successfully engaged in highly optimized localized work. However, in deeply constrained environments where work definitively leads to death (e.g., `decay_04`), its predictive threshold (`<= 0`) caused it to perfectly avoid fatal work actions but oscillate tightly near 0, resting just enough to survive while doing nearly zero work, whereas E000 worked blindly and died.

### Tier 5 — Boundary Shift
- **Question**: Does the Critical Recovery Threshold (CRT) move?
- **Metric**: CRT across constraint ladders.
- **Result**: **NO**. Despite accurately predicting the immediate consequences of its actions, E008 failed to shift the Critical Recovery Threshold. At `gain_01`, `cost_04`, and `decay_00`, it collapsed into the exact same Comatose State or death as the baseline E000 actor.

## Conclusion & Implications
We reject the boundary-shift alternative hypothesis and formally conclude: **Accurate local prediction improves action selection but does not alter asymptotic environmental constraints.**

E008 marks a conceptual milestone in the research program: **The actor has transitioned from observing the system to simulating the system.** It "knows" exactly what will happen if it works or rests on the next tick. Yet, this foresight is structurally insufficient. Because it only looks one step ahead, it cannot string together the multi-step sequences of `REST -> REST -> REST -> WORK` required to efficiently exploit highly constrained resource boundaries. E008 proves that *anticipating the immediate future is not enough to escape the present constraints.*
