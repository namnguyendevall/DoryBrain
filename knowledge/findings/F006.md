# Finding 006: Path-Dependent State Transitions (Hysteresis) Do Not Alter the Equivalence Region

## Context
Following the discovery of Policy Equivalence Regions (O001) where minimal internal state (E004) failed to alter the phase boundary, Phase 3 evaluated whether increasing computational capability could break this invariance. 

We implemented **E005 (Finite-State Controller)**, granting the actor path-dependent state transitions (true hysteresis). In E005, the same observation (e.g., `resource = 22`) yields a different decision depending on the active state (`NORMAL` -> `WORK`, `RECOVERING` -> `REST`).

## Experiment
Batch 0004 evaluated E005 against E000 (Reactive Baseline) and an isomorphic structural control E005_Control (clock-driven FSM). We tracked the **Critical Recovery Threshold (CRT)**, defined as the lowest environmental rest gain parameter where the actor maintains a steady-state work rate $> 0.05$.

## Results
The boundary remained perfectly invariant across all tested policy classes:
- **ladder_gain**: All actors (E000, E004, E005, E005_Control) succumbed to the Comatose Phase at exactly `gain_01` (Survival = 100, SSWorkR = 0.0, Comatose = 1.0).
- **ladder_decay**: All actors collapsed identically at `decay_02`.

## Implication
This provides strong, controlled experimental evidence that **path-dependent state transitions implemented through a finite-state controller are insufficient to escape the Policy Equivalence Region.** 

The boundary dynamics induced by environmental physics (where $rest\_gain \le passive\_decay$ at the critical threshold) continue to dominate the policy's hysteresis logic. The structural capability of hysteresis (Level 1 Expressiveness) does not increase functional resilience at the limit. 

Within the evaluated environments and metrics, no measurable shift in CRT was observed. This rules out simple state-machine memory as a candidate for boundary shifting and necessitates testing higher levels of policy expressiveness (e.g., Continuous Internal Estimators or Sliding Window Memory).
