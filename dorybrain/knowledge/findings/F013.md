# Finding 013: Causal Authority and the Activation Boundary

## Experiment Design
Batch 0010 (E011) tested the hypothesis that giving the agent "causal authority" over its environment—the ability to expend a large amount of resources (cost=30) to permanently improve `rest_gain` (E011-A) or reduce `passive_decay` (E011-B)—would shift the Critical Resource Threshold (CRT) phase boundary.

The agent policy was explicitly designed to prioritize saving up capital (waiting until `resource >= 32`) to invest in environmental improvements before resuming normal work operations.

## Results
1. **The Phase Boundary Can Be Relocated**: In environments where the agent could accumulate capital (e.g., LADDER_WORK, where `rest_gain > passive_decay`), both E011-A and E011-B successfully exercised their causal authority. This permanently altered the environmental physics (increasing net rest gain from +1 to +2). As a result, the steady-state work rate equations changed from `SSWorkR = 1/cost` to `SSWorkR = 2/cost`, significantly extending the theoretical CRT boundary from `cost ≈ 20` to `cost ≈ 40`.

2. **The Two-Layered Boundary Concept**: E011 reveals that the environmental boundary is not a single line, but rather consists of two distinct layers:
   - **Layer 1: Sustainability Boundary**: Does the environment permit the agent to survive? (The traditional CRT).
   - **Layer 2: Activation Boundary**: Does the environment permit the agent to accumulate the capital necessary to intervene? (The newly discovered AT - Activation Threshold).

3. **The Activation Singularity**: In LADDER_GAIN and LADDER_DECAY, the boundary occurs precisely where `rest_gain <= passive_decay` (e.g., `gain_01` where gain=1, decay=1). At these boundaries, the agent's net resource accumulation while resting is $\le 0$. Consequently, the agent cannot accumulate the upfront capital (30 resources) required to invest. Because the agent cannot breach the Activation Threshold, its causal power remains locked. At these specific singularities, the boundary remains **strictly invariant** between E000, E011-A, and E011-B.

## Conclusion
Causal authority (the ability to modify the environment's parameters) **can relocate the phase boundary when the environment permits capability activation.** 

This establishes the most crucial causal distinction of the entire CSRP:
- **Intelligence** determines how well an agent operates inside a fixed world (Phase 1-3).
- **Causal Authority** determines whether the world itself can be changed (Phase 4).

However, an agent is ultimately limited not just by its intelligence or latent causal power, but by the **Activation Constraint**: the ability to reach the threshold of power exertion. If the environment forbids the accumulation of capital, the causal authority is unreachable, and the boundary remains absolute.

### New Metric: Activation Threshold (AT)
- **CRT (Critical Resource Threshold)**: Work sustainability limit.
- **AT (Activation Threshold)**: Intervention accessibility limit (Minimum environmental condition where agent can accumulate enough capital to execute causal intervention).
