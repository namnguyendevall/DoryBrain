# F004: Functional Persistence Boundary of Memoryless Policies

## Evidence Scope
- **Protocol**: CSRP-0001 v0.1.0
- **Batch**: 0002
- **Constraint Families**: Work Ladder, Recovery Ladder, Decay Ladder
- **Policies**: E000 (T=20), E001 (T=30), E002 (Periodic), E003 (Probabilistic), Control
- **Replications**: 100 per configuration

## Observed Patterns

### 1. Survival / Function Divergence (The Comatose State)
In harsh constraint regions (e.g., `gain_01` where `rest_gain <= passive_decay`), threshold policies successfully maintained `survival = 100` but their `work_rate` plummeted towards 0. 
- **E000 (`gain_01`)**: Survival = 100, Work Rate = 0.02, Comatose Fraction = 100%
- **E001 (`gain_01`)**: Survival = 100, Work Rate = 0.00, Comatose Fraction = 100%

*Observation: Functionality is lost before persistence fails. Systems enter a "comatose" regime.*

### 2. Threshold Sensitivity
Raising the safety margin (Threshold 30 vs Threshold 20) did not prevent the functional collapse, but rather exacerbated it across stable regions.
- **`gain_02`**: E000 Work Rate = 0.19  |  E001 Work Rate = 0.17
- **`gain_03`**: E000 Work Rate = 0.30  |  E001 Work Rate = 0.29

*Observation: Higher safety margins trade functional throughput for persistence stability, uniformly depressing the work rate.*

### 3. Open-loop Control Failure
The periodic policy (E002), which does not observe `resource` but instead acts based on `tick`, consistently failed to achieve persistence across all tested ranges.
- **`gain_05` (easiest)**: Survival = 42
- **`gain_01` (hardest)**: Survival = 10

*Observation: Under the tested constraint sets, open-loop periodic control failed to maintain persistent operation compared with observation-driven policies.*

### 4. Random Policy Boundary
Adding stochasticity to the decision threshold (E003) did not improve the boundary limits compared to the strict threshold (E000).
- **`gain_03`**: 
  - E000: Survival = 100.00, Work Rate = 0.300
  - E003: Survival = 98.08, Work Rate = 0.307

*Observation: Randomness introduces survival variance (risk of death) without meaningfully increasing throughput or breaking the functional collapse. It merely shifts the distribution of outcomes.*

---

## Interpretation

The data demonstrates that for **the tested memoryless, reactive policies**, persistence and functionality are bounded by the same constraint geometry. When the environment tightens, the only mathematically viable strategy for these stateless agents to survive is to abandon productive work. No variation of the tested memoryless architectures (higher threshold, stochasticity, or open-loop) was able to break this trade-off. 

## Rejected Explanations
- **Threshold too low**: Rejected for tested range. E001 (higher threshold) suffered the same comatose fate with even lower throughput. Threshold acts merely as a trade-off parameter between risk and productivity.
- **Lack of stochasticity**: Rejected. E003 (probabilistic) added risk without extending the functional boundary.
- **Throughput metric flawed**: Rejected. Both the total productive work and tick-normalized work rate consistently capture the collapse.

## Remaining Uncertainty
Within the evaluated memoryless policies, persistence can be achieved without maintaining functional throughput. Does introducing internal state (e.g., memory of recent actions) alter this persistence-function trade-off under identical constraints?
