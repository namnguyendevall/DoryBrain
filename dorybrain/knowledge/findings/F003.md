# Finding: The Persistence Phase Boundary (F003)
**Date**: 2026-07-28
**Protocol**: CSRP-0001 v0.1.0

## Context
Batch 0001 executed 3,600 simulations to identify the constraint boundaries under which the `Threshold Policy` (E000) ceases to maintain persistent operation compared to the `Random Policy` (Control). Three constraint ladders were tested: `work_cost` (Ladder A), `rest_gain` (Ladder B), and `passive_decay` (Ladder C).

## Synthesis of Findings

### 1. Absolute Robustness to Work Cost (Ladder A)
The Threshold Policy demonstrated a 100% survival rate across the entire `work_cost` ladder (cost 4 through 9). 
- As `work_cost` increased, the policy's decision entropy steadily decreased (from 0.7015 to 0.4690), indicating a behavioral shift toward higher rest frequency. 
- **Conclusion**: As long as a single action does not instantaneously deplete the resource pool to 0, the Threshold Policy acts as a perfect homeostatic buffer, absorbing arbitrary action costs by dilating its recovery period. 

### 2. The Comatose State (Net Zero Boundary)
A distinct phase state emerges when `rest_gain == passive_decay` (observed in `gain_01` and `decay_02`):
- Survival remains at 100%.
- Entropy plummets to ~0.14.
- In this state, the agent exhausts its initial surplus until it falls below the `upper_bound` threshold. From then on, it chooses `Rest` indefinitely. Because net recovery is exactly 0, the agent never reaches the threshold to act again, nor does it lose resources to die. It achieves biological "persistence" but ceases all functional output (a "comatose" state).

### 3. The Lethal Phase Transition (Negative Net Recovery)
The system undergoes a hard phase transition when `rest_gain < passive_decay` (observed in `gain_00`, `decay_03`, `decay_04`, `decay_05`).
- The survival rate abruptly drops from 100% to 0%. 
- The lifespan of the agent becomes completely deterministic (Variance = 0.00 across all 100 seeds for E000). For example, at `gain_00`, E000 dies at exactly tick 21 across all seeds.
- In this lethal region, no policy can survive indefinitely because the environment extracts more resources continuously than can ever be replenished.

## Summary
The boundary of persistent operation for the Threshold Policy is not defined by the cost of its actions, but strictly by the condition:
`rest_gain > passive_decay`

This provides a definitive answer to Q003: The threshold policy maintains persistent operation across all constraint sets where the environment permits a positive net recovery rate, entirely neutralizing the impact of high action costs.
