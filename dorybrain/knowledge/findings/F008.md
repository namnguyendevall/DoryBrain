# Finding 008: Transient–Boundary Decoupling (Behavioral Sensitivity vs Phase Invariance)

## Phenomenon Overview
A policy may measurably alter transient trajectories (e.g., changing early work rate or short-term survival patterns) while leaving the long-term emergent phase boundary (Critical Recovery Threshold, CRT) strictly unchanged.

## Evidence from Batch 0005
During the evaluation of E006 (Sliding Window Memory) against the baseline E000, we observed exactly this decoupling:

1. **Transient Behavior Changed**: At the critical boundary step `gain_01`, the finite-history controller (E006) successfully recognized the starvation constraint and actively engaged its lowered threshold mode (79% of the time). This produced a measurable change in the transient behavior—the average initial work rate marginally increased from $0.02$ to $0.03$.
2. **Phase Boundary Unchanged**: Despite this behavioral response and increased transient work, the long-term **steady-state work rate** collapsed to $0.00$ exactly as it did in the baseline. The actor entered the Comatose State with 100% survival, leaving the CRT completely invariant.

## Implication
This decoupling demonstrates that the system operates on two distinct dynamic levels:
- **Local/Transient Dynamics**: Susceptible to policy adjustments (e.g., short-term memory, threshold shifting).
- **Asymptotic Phase Dynamics (Boundary)**: Governed by fundamental environmental physics ($rest\_gain \le passive\_decay$) and highly resistant to structural policy changes unless those changes alter the asymptotic recovery curve itself.

Therefore, improving an actor's transient behavior in a constraint space does not guarantee an escape from the Policy Equivalence Region. Any future candidate capability must demonstrate a shift in the steady-state CRT, not merely a transient improvement.
