# F005: Boundary Invariance Under Minimal Internal State

## Statement
Introducing a minimal adaptive internal state (ticks_since_last_work) successfully altered the agent's internal policy state, but did not alter the emergent phase boundary.

The agent correctly detected prolonged functional starvation and repeatedly entered starvation mode by lowering its work threshold.

However, under environmental regimes where the net resource dynamics prevented recovery, this adaptive response produced no measurable improvement in work rate, survival, or comatose fraction.

These results indicate that the observed boundary is dominated by environmental dynamics rather than by the absence of short-term adaptive internal state.

## Context
- Batch: 0003
- Policies: E000 (Reactive), E004_Control (Deterministic Schedule), E004 (Adaptive Starvation State)
- Protocol: CSRP-0001 v0.1.0

## Falsified Alternatives
- **Hypothesis A**: The Comatose boundary is caused by the rigidity of a fixed threshold.
  - *Falsified by*: E004_Control, which varied the threshold deterministically over time but resulted in an emergent boundary consistent with the fixed-threshold baseline.
- **Hypothesis B**: The Comatose boundary occurs because the actor lacks internal awareness of its own functional starvation.
  - *Falsified by*: E004, which tracked its functional starvation state and dynamically adapted its threshold, yet still failed to break the boundary.

## Future Work
Future work should investigate whether richer internal mechanisms—including long-horizon memory, predictive estimation, or explicit planning—can alter these boundaries, or whether the boundary remains invariant under increasingly expressive policy classes.
