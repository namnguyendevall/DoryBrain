# Finding 007: Finite-History Controllers and Boundary Invariance

## Overview
**Date**: 2026-07-29
**Experiment**: E006 (Batch 0005)
**Hypotheses Tested**: 
- $H_D$ (Diagnostic): The sliding-window state is actively engaged during evaluated trajectories.
- $H_0$: Sliding-window memory does not measurably shift the Critical Recovery Threshold (CRT).

## Setup
We evaluated an **E006 Finite-History Controller**, which maintains a sliding window of $N=10$ ticks tracking productive work occurrence (work density). If work density over the window falls to zero, it lowers its internal threshold from 20 to 15. This explicitly avoids the shielded variable trap (where failures aren't observed during starvation) by tracking work over *time*.

An isomorphic control, **E006_Control**, applied the same threshold logic but filled its queue with a deterministic clock signal rather than semantic observations.

## Results
- **Diagnostic Verification ($H_D$)**: At the critical constraint boundaries (e.g., `gain_01`, `decay_02`), E006 spent **79% of its time** in the lowered threshold state (`LowThresh = 0.79`). This confirms the capability was actively engaged and heavily utilized.
- **Boundary Shift ($H_0$)**: Despite the active engagement of the finite history memory, the CRT was strictly invariant.
  - In `ladder_gain`: E000, E006, and E006_Control all collapsed into the Comatose state (100% survival, 0.00 steady-state work rate) at exactly `gain_01`.
  - In `ladder_decay`: All evaluated actors collapsed identically at `decay_02`.
- **Transient Behavior**: E006 exhibited only trivial transient variations (e.g., overall work rate shifted from 0.02 to 0.03 at `gain_01`), but its long-term functional throughput (steady-state work rate) dropped to zero identically to the baseline.

## Implication
This provides controlled experimental evidence that **the evaluated finite-history aggregation (sliding window memory) is insufficient to escape the Policy Equivalence Region.** 

Even when the internal state is explicitly and successfully triggered by the collapse into comatose dynamics, the evaluated reactive policy parameterized by a short-term finite history cannot overcome the environmental physics boundary where $rest\_gain \le passive\_decay$. 

We must proceed to test continuous estimators or predictive models to see if richer historical integration or forward-looking dynamics can break this equivalence.
